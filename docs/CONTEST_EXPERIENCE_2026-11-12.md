# Contest experience — November 12, 2026

Organizer-reported experience, recorded for reuse in later events. This note
captures the teaching and running sequence supplied by the organizer; it does
not assert team counts, performance measurements or final results. The current
[runbook](CONTEST_RUNBOOK.md) turns it into an actionable checklist.

## Sequence to repeat

1. **Introduce the challenge.** Start with the game and what a strategy should
   achieve, before explaining implementation details.
2. **Distribute documentation and login files.** Give every team the student
   materials and its own printed login card, so everyone can get started together.
3. **Ask teams to enter names in their profiles.** This connects numbered accounts
   to the students and makes it easier for organizers to help the right team.
4. **Ask every team to download and submit the sample code, such as `B.py`.**
   Establish a common working starting point before teams develop strategies.
5. **Explain the technical implementation.** Walk through the turn loop, input
   and output protocol, valid responses and execution limits using that sample.
6. **Show the submission section’s step-by-step debugger.** Have teams inspect
   each step and use it to understand and debug their own changes.
7. **Run tournaments frequently.** First get the initial runs stable; then run
   practice tournaments about once every ten minutes so teams see feedback as
   their programs improve.
8. **Run the final in stages.** Finish a tournament, qualify a subset of its
   leading teams, and repeat step by step until the final stage.

## Lessons for the next event

Keep onboarding in that order: login and profile first, a working sample next,
then technical details and debugging. Frequent practice gives students feedback
before the final; introduce the ten-minute cadence only once the setup is stable.
Make qualification explicit and announce stage sizes before the final begins.

For current platform operation, the runbook adds queue-capacity checks, completed
source tournaments, the minimum games needed to rank, source-freezing behavior,
private print-and-cut cards, and complete backups. These are operating guidance
for future events, rather than additional claims about what happened on this date.
